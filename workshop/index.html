What kind of Nether maze do you want?

You are the designer. Try a route, say what happened, then change one thing. These little chase maps help you choose; the final Nether layers, breakable walls and hammer come later.

1. Pick a way to try it

Start wherever you like. You can also choose any map above.

Reference maze

Hold and drag on the maze. Dodge is on.

Blue = you. Gold flags = places to try reaching. Gray pen = waiting monsters. Colored outline = a changed wall or path.

Try, notice, choose

Follow the finger + dodge on. Same chase speeds and release timing on every map. A catch returns you to the start; you can keep trying. Alive time does not pick the best design.

In your own sketch, tap a square to change wall ↔ path. Start, flags, pen and outside edge stay put.

2. What should a new attempt be like?

A mirror keeps the same shape of challenge but moves things around. New routes can change the challenge more. Neither option has to win today.

3. Make the layers feel fair

If two maps have the same layer, what should feel equally challenging? Dad can help write; you decide what the challenge is.

The paper plan does not change the chase sketch. Add only the layers you want. Stronger walls and upgrades are already part of your Nether idea; how you earn each upgrade is still a separate choice.

Dad’s comparison helpers

Compare the same stage in each layout: how far to the next goal, escape routes, dead ends, wall-opening choices, monster release pressure and room to see threats. Similar numbers are a clue, not proof of equal difficulty.

4. Keep your idea

You can choose a favourite, mix ideas, reject all of them, or say “not yet.” You do not need to fill in every box. Dad can ask the questions aloud.
